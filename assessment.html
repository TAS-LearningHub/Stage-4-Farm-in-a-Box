Canonical student design brief · version 1.0

Farm in a Box: Grow More with Less

Situation: Useful agriculture can happen in a small space. The challenge is to create a compact crop-production system that makes careful use of water and other resources, can be monitored with simple records, and can be improved when conditions change.

Brief: Design, establish, monitor, improve and evaluate a portable Farm in a Box crop-production system using the crop, location, materials, tools and processes approved by your teacher. Use evidence to show how the system supports plant growth, manages resources and could be improved for another growing cycle.

Required design and evidence features

  • a clearly defined need, intended users and measurable success criteria;
  • research into the selected crop and its growing requirements;
  • at least two genuinely different annotated design ideas;
  • a justified final design and production plan;
  • teacher-approved safe production and establishment;
  • a field record containing dates, observations, measurements and decisions;
  • at least one evidence-based test or modification; and
  • a final judgement against the criteria and a specific next-version proposal.

Criteria for judging the system

  • Function
    Does it perform its intended crop-support function under the available conditions?
  • Resource use
    Are water, materials and crop resources used carefully and justified?
  • Safe production
    Was the approved project produced and maintained using the teacher’s current local controls?
  • Manageability
    Can it be monitored and maintained within the available time, place and care arrangements?
  • Communication
    Are decisions explained clearly with relevant evidence?
  • Improvement
    Did testing lead to a specific evidence-based improvement or next-version proposal?

Fixed boundaries

  • The unit uses 25 one-hour periods across approximately ten weeks.
  • Manual observations and measurements are enough; sensors are optional enrichment.
  • The harvested material can be measured, graded and photographed without being eaten.
  • Crop failure, delayed growth or disrupted access becomes evidence for diagnosis and redesign.
  • Animals, aquaponics, permanent structures, mains-powered systems, pesticides and fertiliser instructions are not assumed.

Your teacher still confirms the crop, site, materials, tools, practical process, local safety controls, care arrangements and any individual evidence requirements.

Equivalent evidence pathway

When the crop does not grow as planned

Biology is variable. Unexpected growth is not automatic project failure; it becomes a chance to make a careful, evidence-proportional decision.

  1. Observe
    Describe what can actually be seen or measured.
  2. Record
    Add the date, conditions, method and a labelled image or teacher-supplied fallback record.
  3. Compare
    Use the baseline, another observation or a consistent data set.
  4. Diagnose carefully
    List more than one plausible explanation and state what remains unknown.
  5. Recommend
    Choose one low-risk investigation or teacher-approved change.
  6. Re-check
    Collect comparable evidence and propose the next version without guaranteeing a result.

Fallback evidence: when germination, weather, access or holidays interrupt production, the teacher may provide an image sequence or data set so you can complete the same observation, comparison, diagnosis and redesign thinking.
